The problem

A shoreline is not a snapshot.

Coastlines rarely fail all at once. Sediment shifts. Channels migrate. Erosion accelerates in places no one happens to be watching. Structures that were stable for years become exposed in months.

Conventional surveys capture a moment — one pass, one tide, one season. They are accurate, and still incomplete. TIDELINE is built to reveal what happens in between: the slow accumulation that a single visit can't show.

How it works

From field conditions to a decision your team can act on.

TIDELINE carries a single observation through four stages, so every decision can be traced back to what was actually measured.

Field conditions. Sensors and survey passes record raw, noisy signal directly from the coastline — water level, wave energy, shoreline position.

Measurement. Raw signal is cleaned and aligned against prior passes, producing a consistent, comparable profile of the coastline.

Interpretation. The model compares passes over time and flags where change is concentrated and where it departs from the expected trend.

Decision. Flagged change becomes a specific, boundable recommendation — a corridor to inspect, a structure to re-survey, a priority to set.

Project example

North Breakwater / Pilot 07

North Atlantic commercial harbour · 18‑month monitoring period

Dredging at this harbour had been scheduled from periodic survey data, which treated the full approach channel as uniformly unstable. Eighteen months of continuous observation showed otherwise.

Sediment movement was concentrated in a corridor narrower than the surveyed approach suggested. The engineering team redirected inspection and dredging activity toward the corridor that was actually changing, instead of treating the entire approach as equally at risk.

Operating principle

More data is not the product.
Better decisions are.

TIDELINE exists to help engineering teams understand three things clearly: what changed, where it changed, and what deserves attention first. Everything in the system is built back from that.
