COASTLINE

The problem

A shoreline is not a snapshot.

Erosion, sediment movement and shifting water conditions change the geometry of a coastline — and what it exposes — continuously. A single survey, a single flyover, a single report is a still frame of a system that never stops moving. Decisions built on isolated observations inherit their blind spots.

  • Shoreline geometry shifts between surveys, not only during them.
  • Isolated readings can't distinguish normal variation from a developing trend.
  • Infrastructure exposure changes with the coast it stands on.

OBSERVATION

System

Observe. Interpret. Act.

TIDELINE's pipeline turns raw physical readings into operational understanding — the same corridor, read continuously rather than re-surveyed from scratch each time.

  1. Field
  2. Measurements
  3. Interpretation
  4. Decision

Observe

Distributed sensors, bathymetric passes and field survey feed one corridor-level record instead of disconnected site visits.

Interpret

Readings are reconciled against prior observation and predictive modelling, so a single point is read in the context of a trend.

Act

Interpretation resolves into a specific, boundable question: where to inspect, reinforce or re-survey — and where not to.

DECISION

Operating principle

More data is not the product.
Better decisions are.

Every sensor TIDELINE adds has to earn its place in a decision, not just in a dashboard. We build toward the smallest corridor of attention that answers the question a team actually has.

About

Built for people who are accountable for a coastline.

TIDELINE is built for ports, coastal engineers and infrastructure teams who need to justify where attention and budget go — not for a general audience scrolling past a dashboard. The system is deliberately boring where it should be: traceable inputs, legible interpretation, and a record you can defend in a planning meeting.
